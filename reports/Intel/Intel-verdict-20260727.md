## Verdict: 🔴 AVOID

**One-line reason:** Intel is a capital-destroying turnaround story in a business Buffett would never touch — negative free cash flow, an eroded moat, and no reliable earnings base to even calculate a margin of safety.

---

## Business Summary

| | |
|---|---|
| **What it does** | Designs and (increasingly) manufactures semiconductors — CPUs for PCs/servers (its legacy cash cow) and, since 2021, a foundry business (Intel Foundry) built to compete with TSMC. The bull case rests on Intel's 18A/14A process nodes closing the manufacturing gap with TSMC, backed by CHIPS Act funding |
| **Moat** | Eroding fast. Intel lost process leadership to TSMC years ago, ceded high-margin server share to AMD, and missed the AI accelerator wave entirely to Nvidia. Its historical moat (best-in-class fabs + x86 lock-in) is now a liability — massive fixed-cost fabs running below utilization |
| **10-year outlook** | Binary. Either 18A/14A execution works and Intel Foundry becomes a genuine TSMC alternative (helped by government/strategic capital), or Intel continues bleeding cash while ceding client, server, and AI silicon share. This is a speculative industrial turnaround, not a "wonderful business at a fair price" |

## Valuation Range

No credible intrinsic-value range can be calculated — earnings are negative and free cash flow is negative. Any "valuation" today is a bet on 18A/14A yields and foundry customer wins, not a cash-flow-based estimate. This alone is disqualifying under R-InvestIQ's price-discipline rule.

---

## Top Risks

1. **Execution risk on 18A/14A:** The entire investment case depends on Intel Foundry hitting yield and cost targets it has repeatedly missed on prior nodes (10nm, 7nm delays).
2. **Structural share loss:** AMD (servers/PCs) and Nvidia (AI accelerators) have taken durable share in Intel's highest-margin segments, and there is no clear path back.
